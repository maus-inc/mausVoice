One dictation crosses the same stages every time, and the pill is the only surface that reports each one. When something looks wrong, read the pill first. It tells you whether the app is capturing, converting, delivering, or finished.

This guide follows a single dictation from the key press to the text in the target field. The [dictation workflow reference](/mausVoice/docs/using-mausvoice/dictation-workflow/) states the same stages as a list. This page shows them.

The three states are mutually exclusive. A pill that stays in Transcribing has not delivered anything yet, and a pill that returns to idle without a Done confirmation did not finish a delivery.

## Walk through one dictation

<Steps>
  <Step
    title="Hold the dictation shortcut"
    image="dictation-step-1-hold"
    alt="The macOS, Windows, and Linux default hold-to-dictate shortcuts"
  >
    The defaults are `Fn` on macOS, Left Windows/Meta plus Left Control on Windows, and Left Control plus Left Alt plus Space on Linux. Dictation is a hold action: the press starts capture and the release stops it. A saved custom binding replaces the default once it exists, and the reset button restores the platform default. The [default shortcuts reference](/mausVoice/docs/reference/default-shortcuts/) lists the full set, including the actions that ship with no key at all.
  </Step>
  <Step
    title="Speak while the pill shows Listening"
    image="dictation-step-2-speak"
    alt="The pill in the recording state with a live capture waveform"
  >
    Capture uses the microphone selected in **Settings → Dictation → Microphone**. With **Real-time output** enabled, committed segments are inserted while you are still speaking. Without it, nothing reaches the target until you release. If the pill never leaves idle, the shortcut did not reach the app. The [hotkey troubleshooting guide](/mausVoice/docs/troubleshooting/hotkeys/) separates a reserved combination from a permission problem.
  </Step>
  <Step
    title="Release to stop and pick an engine"
    image="dictation-step-3-release"
    alt="The pill in the transcribing state with the available transcription engines"
  >
    Releasing the shortcut ends capture. The audio goes to whichever engine the transcription settings select: the local sidecar running Whisper, Parakeet, Canary, or SenseVoice, or an API provider. Local engines keep audio on the device. A network provider sends the audio to that provider under its own retention terms, which the [data flow page](/mausVoice/docs/privacy-security/data-flow/) states per provider.
  </Step>
  <Step
    title="Processing runs in a fixed order"
    image="dictation-step-4-process"
    alt="The processing stages in order: dictionary, silence filter, spoken commands, symbols, post-processing"
  >
    The raw transcript passes dictionary replacements, the silence hallucination filter, spoken commands, symbol conversion, and optional post-processing, in that order. The order matters. A dictionary replacement applies before post-processing, so a style cannot undo it. Post-processing itself is deterministic on the device for a short English prose dictation in a prose style, and goes to the generative provider for longer text, another language, a structured style, or a custom style.
  </Step>
  <Step
    title="The text is delivered to the focused target"
    image="dictation-step-5-deliver"
    alt="Text delivered into the focused field with the pill reporting Inserted"
  >
    Paste sends the text through the clipboard. Simulated typing sends keystrokes instead. Focus is read at delivery time, not at the start, so if you click into another field while a network provider is still working, the field with focus when delivery runs receives the text. This is the single most common cause of "it typed into the wrong place".
  </Step>
</Steps>

A failed insertion does not imply a failed transcription. The text may already be in History or on the clipboard. Check both before you change any provider setting, then work through focus, permissions, the paste shortcut, and the typing delay in that order.

## Cancel and pause

The pill carries two side controls. Pause and resume sit on the left, cancel sits on the right, and both sit fully outside the pill body so a press on the pill itself still means the pill. Pressing **Escape** uses the cancel-transcription shortcut and produces the same result as the cancel control.

<Callout title="Cancelling is not the same as a provider failure">
  A cancel ends the session on purpose and leaves no diagnostic evidence. A provider failure leaves a failed status and a reason you can act on. The [troubleshooting first response](/mausVoice/docs/troubleshooting/first-response/) separates the two before you change any settings.
</Callout>

## What is stored

Unless Incognito is on, the app stores the transcription record and attempts to keep its audio snapshot. Automatic cleanup keeps managed audio for only the 20 newest rows that have audio, so a transcription older than that keeps its text and loses its audio. Incognito skips the saved row entirely, and **Include incognito in stats** decides whether the word count still reaches the Home heatmap.

## When review is enabled

With **Review before insert** on, the transcript stops before delivery and opens in the pill's editable panel. Insert, Copy, Open, and Cancel decide what happens next, and an unanswered review expires after five minutes. The [review guide](/mausVoice/docs/guides/transcript-review/) walks through all five outcomes, the queue, and the expiry.
